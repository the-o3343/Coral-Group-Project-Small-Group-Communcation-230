Fuel Your Future Without Draining Your Wallet

Food insecurity is the lack of consistent access to enough affordable, nutritious food for an active, healthy life. It can range from worrying about having enough food to eat to actually skipping meals or going without food because of limited money or resources. For college students, food insecurity means that a student may not always have enough money or resources to obtain the food they need.

Students may experience food insecurity because of high tuition and housing costs, limited income, lack of transportation, or unexpected expenses. As a result, they might skip meals, eat less nutritious foods, rely on food pantries or meal programs, or choose between buying food and paying for necessities such as rent, textbooks, or utilities.Food insecurity can also affect students' physical health, mental well-being, academic performance, and ability to succeed in college.

What factors contribute to food insecurity?

Several factors can contribute to food insecurity among college students in the Vancouver/Portland metropolitan area. Often, students experience several of these challenges at the same time:

  • Tuition and education expenses: Tuition, textbooks, fees, and other school costs can leave less money available for groceries.
  • High housing costs: Rent and utilities take up a large portion of many students' budgets. When housing costs rise, food is often one of the expenses students cut back on.
  • Transportation costs: Gas, public transportation, car payments, and maintenance can make it harder to afford food, especially for students who commute to campus.
  • Inflation and rising food prices: Increases in the cost of groceries and other necessities reduce students' purchasing power.
  • Limited work hours: Full-time students may not be able to work enough hours to cover their living expenses, particularly when balancing classes, studying, and employment.
  • Low or unpredictable income: Part-time jobs, seasonal employment, or inconsistent work schedules can make it difficult to maintain a reliable food budget.
  • Financial aid limitations: Grants, scholarships, and loans may not fully cover living expenses, and students may face delays or gaps in financial assistance.
  • Family and caregiving responsibilities: Students supporting children, relatives, or other dependents have additional expenses that can compete with their food budget.
  • Limited access to affordable food: Students without reliable transportation, cooking facilities, or nearby grocery stores may have fewer affordable and nutritious food options.
  • Unexpected expenses: Medical bills, car repairs, housing emergencies, or other unexpected costs can quickly consume money that was intended for groceries.
