What is SNAP? 
SNAP, or the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, is a federal program that 
helps people with lower incomes pay for groceries. In Washington, SNAP is called 
Basic Food. This can be helpful for college students who are struggling to afford 
food while going to school. There are also some extra requirements for college 
students. In Washington, students ages 18–49 who are enrolled at least half-time 
usually need to meet an additional requirement to qualify. For example, a student 
may qualify if they work an average of 20 hours a week, participate in a state or 
federally funded work-study program, receive TANF, or meet certain childcare 
requirements.
Who is most affected by food insecurity in the Vancouver/Portland metropolitan area?

In the Vancouver/Portland metropolitan area, college students most affected by food insecurity tend to be those facing low incomes and other barriers to stable resources. Local and regional data point to several groups.

Low-income students: Financial pressure from tuition, rent, transportation, and other expenses makes food insecurity more likely. Washington's 2024 survey found basic-needs insecurity among 70% of students with low incomes.

First-generation college students: Students whose parents did not complete a bachelor's degree experience higher rates of basic-needs insecurity. Washington reported a 63% rate among first-generation students in 2024 (Student Supports).

Black, Indigenous, and other students of color: These students experience disproportionately high rates. Washington data found particularly high insecurity among American Indian/Alaska Native and Black students.

Students with children or dependents: Childcare and household expenses can make it difficult to afford food. Washington reported basic-needs insecurity among 68% of students with dependents.

Students with disabilities: Students with disabilities also experience higher rates of basic-needs insecurity, with Washington reporting 65% in 2024.

Former foster youth and students who have experienced homelessness: These students are among the most vulnerable groups. Washington reported basic-needs insecurity among 84% of students who had experienced foster care or homelessness in high school (Student Supports).
